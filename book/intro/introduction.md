## What is health data?

Health data represents the diverse set of information a person’s[^1] physical or mental health, their interactions with the healthcare system, as well as lifestyle factors that may impact health in the future. There are many different streams of health data, and the unification of these streams has been a goal of forward-thinking biomedical scientists for decades.[^2]

The various types of health data include information collected during traditional healthcare encounters in doctors offices and hospitals include the electronic health record (EHR) which holds clinician’s written observations and laboratory tests results, medical imaging like X-rays and MRIs, and molecular sequencing (DNA & RNA). A growing share of health data is collected *outside* the clinic entirely such as patient-generated data from wearables, home monitoring devices, and mobile apps. Administrative claims generated by insurers are considered health data because they contain information about each patient’s type of care and semi-reliable information about diagnoses. Clinical trials generate carefully structured data collected under research protocols, while molecular data from genomic and other sequencing technologies describes patients at the biological level.

As you can see, health data is complex and varied and these streams often provide complementary information on an individual. Understanding the role of each in the biomedical enterprise is not challenging but does require some effort - hence this book!

## Who should read this book

I wrote this book because I found existing materials too academic and wanted to provide a no-nonsense, practical guide with just the bare minimum of information to get readers fluent in the language of all the distinct health data streams. Regardless of whether you are a data scientist who pushes two commits per day to Github, an executive who wants to speak the language of a new domain, or an entrepreneur looking for their next app idea, this book can help you.

This book is perfect for mid-career tech professionals who want to transition into healthcare from another industry.  If this is you, then you’re in the right place and I’m glad you are here. Welcoming technologists with a finance, retail, or manufacturing background into healthcare delivery and technology organizations is critical, given that 28% of health systems report that the lack of health professionals with data science skills is preventing them from becoming more data driven.[^3]

This book is also for data analysts and data scientists in healthcare who want to expand their knowledge. I have worked with PhDs who spent their entire careers writing software genomic sequencing data and have no understanding of the clinical data within Electronic Health Record systems (EHRs). I have also worked with career state employees who could list off every detail of a standard medical claims extract but could not tell you a single fact about EHR data. If you work within *any* healthcare domain, you know that this is the *rule* rather than the *exception*. However, if we are to modernize medicine we must foster a more expansive view of health data so that we can do things such as understand the real-world clinical impact of gene therapies or fully represent a patient’s interactions with the health system.

## How to read this book

Each chapter in this book represents a distinct *stream* of healthcare data. - clinical data is generated by health systems, claims data by insurers, and molecular sequencing data by sequencing machines. Because each data stream in healthcare has its own ecosystem and methods for analysis, each chapter is atomic and can be read by itself. If you’d like to establish an encyclopedic breadth of the various types of healthcare data streams, then by all means, read this book cover-to-cover. However, this book was designed to serve as a reference text also so simply find the data stream you are looking for in the Table of Contents.
